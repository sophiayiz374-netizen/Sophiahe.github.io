01 / LISTENING

I learned to listen before people had words.

Animals cannot explain their needs in human language. Years of caring for cats and dogs, and fostering rescue cats in Toronto, taught me to notice subtle signals, stay patient, and look beyond what is said.

That habit follows me into design. I pay attention to hesitation, workarounds, and the small moments that reveal what people actually need.

observe first. interpret carefully. respond clearly.

quiet signals become visible when I slow down

02 / CONTEXT

38 countries. One hundred cities. No single way to design for everyone.

Travel taught me that people understand spaces, services, and information through culture and context. Museums, galleries, public collections, and city streets shaped how I think about wayfinding, accessibility, and storytelling.

I collect visual clues from every place I visit.

03 / OBSERVATION

Photography trained me to notice what others might miss.

I use photography to study composition, light, rhythm, and the small details that give a place its character. This practice sharpens the same visual judgment I bring to UX: deciding what deserves attention and how a story should unfold.
